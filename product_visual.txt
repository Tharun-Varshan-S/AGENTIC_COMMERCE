Yes. **This is exactly the right question to solve before you code further.** There are actually **two separate things**:

1. **How does a merchant integrate with your AI-commerce system?**
2. **Why would a customer use your AI instead of Google Search?**

Let's make both concrete.

---

# 1. How does a merchant actually integrate?

Imagine you have **3,000 Razorpay merchants**.

They should NOT manually come to your platform and enter every product.

Instead, your future system should work like this:

```text
                  MERCHANT
                     │
                     │ already uses
                     ↓
                 RAZORPAY
                     │
                     ↓
             Enable AI Commerce
                     │
          ┌──────────┼──────────┐
          ↓          ↓          ↓
       Catalog     Inventory   Orders
          │          │          │
          └──────────┼──────────┘
                     ↓
             AI Commerce API
                     │
                     ↓
                AI AGENTS
```

The merchant essentially says:

> **"I want my products to be discoverable by AI agents."**

Then their existing product/catalog information gets exposed through standardized APIs.

---

# 2. What exactly does the merchant provide?

Suppose Merchant A sells laptops.

Their system already knows:

```text
Product ID
Name
Price
Inventory
Description
Images
Category
Shipping
Returns
```

Your AI-commerce layer standardizes that.

Something conceptually like:

```json
{
  "merchant_id": "M123",
  "product_id": "LAP456",
  "name": "Dell Inspiron 15",
  "price": 64999,
  "currency": "INR",
  "inventory": 18,
  "delivery": "2-3 days",
  "return_days": 7
}
```

You don't need the merchant to maintain a second catalog.

**That's important.**

---

# 3. Then how does the customer search?

The customer does NOT search each merchant.

They say:

> "Laptop under ₹70,000."

Your AI searches across the participating merchants.

For example:

```text
                    USER
                      │
          "Laptop under ₹70k"
                      │
                      ↓
                  AI AGENT
                      │
                      ↓
              SEARCH/RETRIEVAL
                      │
       ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
       ↓              ↓              ↓
   Merchant A     Merchant B     Merchant C
       │              │              │
       ↓              ↓              ↓
    Offers         Offers         Offers
       └──────────────┼──────────────┘
                      ↓
                AI RANKING
                      ↓
                  RESULTS
```

---

# 4. Now your BIG question:

> **"Google Search under ₹3,000 gives me the same results. So what does AI do?"**

🔥 **This is the heart of your project.**

If your AI simply does:

> "Search products under ₹3,000"

and returns:

```text
Product A ₹2,999
Product B ₹2,799
Product C ₹2,599
```

then **you have built nothing impressive.**

Google already does that.

---

# 5. AI's advantage is understanding the PURCHASE INTENT

Compare these two searches.

### Google:

> "headphones under 3000"

Google primarily treats this as a search/query and returns pages, products, ads, shopping results, etc.

### Your AI:

> "I need headphones under ₹3,000 for online classes. I attend meetings every day, so microphone quality is important. I don't care about gaming."

That's a completely different problem.

Your AI can convert that into:

```text
Budget: ≤ ₹3,000
Use case: Online classes
Priority: Microphone
Priority: Comfort
Gaming: Low priority
```

Then evaluate merchant offers against those requirements.

---

# 6. Even better: AI can ask questions

Google:

> Search → Results

Your system:

> "What's most important to you: sound quality, microphone, or battery?"

User:

> "Microphone."

AI:

> "Do you prefer wireless?"

User:

> "Yes."

Now:

```text
USER INTENT
     ↓
Budget ≤ ₹3,000
     ↓
Wireless
     ↓
Excellent microphone
     ↓
Online meetings
     ↓
AI searches merchant offers
     ↓
AI ranks
```

That's **agentic discovery**, not ordinary keyword search.

---

# 7. And here's the biggest difference

Google can help you **find** a product.

Your agent should help you **complete the task**.

Example:

### User:

> "I need a birthday gift for my brother. He's into gaming. Budget ₹3,000. It has to arrive by Friday."

Your AI:

**Step 1 — Understand**

```text
Gift
Gaming
₹3,000
Deadline: Friday
```

**Step 2 — Discover**

Search participating merchants.

**Step 3 — Compare**

Price + delivery + ratings + returns + suitability.

**Step 4 — Recommend**

> "I'd recommend this gaming headset from Merchant B."

**Step 5**

User:

> "Buy it."

**Step 6**

AI:

> "Payment is ₹2,799. Shall I proceed?"

User:

> "Yes."

**Step 7**

Razorpay handles transaction.

That's the difference.

## One architecture change I strongly recommend

Your backend should eventually have these concepts separately:

```text
Merchant
    ↓
Catalog
    ↓
Product
    ↓
Offer
    ↓
Inventory
    ↓
Order
    ↓
Payment
```

**Product ≠ Offer.**

For example:

```text
PRODUCT
Dell Inspiron 15
       │
       ├── OFFER
       │   Merchant A
       │   ₹65,000
       │
       ├── OFFER
       │   Merchant B
       │   ₹67,000
       │
       └── OFFER
           Merchant C
           ₹63,000
```

That single design decision will make your system much more realistic when you scale from **3 demo merchants → 3,000 merchants**.

And **that** is the version I would build for the Razorpay Track 01 demo.
